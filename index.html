The problem

Big decisions get juggled, not solved

People facing high-stakes decisions often juggle multiple competing criteria in their heads. That leads to one of two places: decision paralysis from overthinking every angle, or a gut-feel choice that can't be explained or revisited later. There's no lightweight tool that forces structure onto a fuzzy decision without requiring a full spreadsheet — until now.

How it works

From "I don't know how to think about this" to a defensible answer, in one sitting

  1. 1

    Name the decision, add your options

    2 to 5 things you're actually choosing between.

  2. 2

    Add criteria and weight what matters

    Up to 8 factors, each weighted 1–10 relative to the others. Smart suggestions kick in based on your decision title.

  3. 3

    Score each option

    Rate every option against every criterion, 1–10. A running weighted total updates as you go.

  4. 4

    Get a recommendation — and the "why"

    A weighted score per option, a clear winner, and a breakdown of exactly which criteria drove it.

  5. 5

    See how robust that answer really is

    Sensitivity analysis shows how much a single weight would need to shift to flip the outcome.

  6. 6

    Log what you actually chose

    Later, revisit the decision, record the real outcome, and build a track record that informs the next one.

More than a spreadsheet

The parts that make it a tool, not a template

Sensitivity analysis

Comparing your top 2 options, the tool solves for exactly how much a criterion's weight would need to move for the runner-up to overtake the leader — live, as you adjust weights. "If you valued Growth potential 67% less, Job A would overtake Job B." Plus a plain-language read on how close the call is.

Decision history that learns, lightly

Once a decision has a recommendation, it's saved automatically. Log the real outcome later, and the tool flags whether it matched. On your next decision, it surfaces passive signals — criteria you've consistently valued, or a pattern of going with your gut over a specific criterion — as suggestions you can ignore, never as an automatic override.

Trade-offs, on purpose

What v1 deliberately gives up

Simplicity vs. flexibility

More criteria and options make the tool more powerful for complex decisions, but increase the setup cost and risk of abandonment. V1 caps options at 5 and criteria at 8 to keep the whole flow usable in one sitting, at the cost of not fully serving very complex decisions.

Personalization vs. trust

Surfacing patterns from past decisions can make recommendations feel smarter, but if it's too aggressive or opaque, it risks feeling like the tool is steering you. V1 keeps history-based signals as passive suggestions you can ignore, never automatic adjustments to your weights or scores.

Non-goals for v1

What's deliberately out of scope

  • Collaborative or shared decision-making (e.g. two people scoring together)
  • More than 5 options or 8 criteria per decision
  • A mobile-native app — responsive web only
  • Proactive coaching or pop-ups based on history — learning only surfaces when you're actively setting up a new decision
